Certifying clinicians

The clinician who certifies a patient's qualifying condition. The Act says clinician; the draft rule says certifying clinician. 7.35.2.7 NMAC defines a clinician as an approved health care provider licensed in New Mexico who holds a department certification to provide medical services to qualified patients, 7.35.2.7 (C)(4), p. 2. Nothing about the role is in effect yet.

What the draft asks of a certifying clinician

Every certification applicant submits legal name, address, telephone, and e-mail; a legible copy of a driver's license or comparable photo identification; documentation of the applicable educational requirements; for renewal, continuing education; consent to publication of contact information, an affirmation that the submission is true and accurate, and an attestation of no sex-offender registration in any jurisdiction; and a signature and date. 7.35.3.9 (C), p. 3

A certifying clinician additionally submits documentation of a current professional license to practice and diagnose in New Mexico, a New Mexico controlled substance number, HIPAA certification completed within the preceding two years, a completed W-9, and whatever else the department reasonably requires. 7.35.3.9 (D), p. 3

Education. The educational requirements for certifying clinicians are at 7.35.3.18, p. 11; certification on the basis of a program from another jurisdiction, and the department's list of approved out-of-state programs, at 7.35.3.10, p. 4; requirements and prohibitions once certified at 7.35.3.13, p. 8.

After filing. The department reviews a completed application and notifies the applicant of approval or denial; a denied applicant may re-apply within six months, and a second denial bars re-application for six months. 7.35.3.9 (A), p. 2. Renewal has its own deadline, 7.35.3.9 (B), p. 3.
