New Mexico medical psilocybin program · producer and laboratory permits under 7.35.2 NMAC

Pathways to a permit

The rule names what an applicant has in hand; these pages say where each item comes from, agency by agency, starting with the City of Santa Fe and Santa Fe County. Every step is cited to the rule or to the page of the agency that administers it, with the date it was read. What the rule leaves unsaid is said to be unsaid.

Created September 7, 2026. Sources read that day. The rules site carries the rule's own list; this site carries the way to each item.

The Department of Health began accepting producer permit applications on August 17, 2026, at mpp.nm.btgov.com, with no deadline and review in order of submission. The rule that governs the permit, 7.35.2 NMAC, has been in effect since June 23, 2026. It asks for twelve records from every applicant and one more from a producer, and it defines none of them; each is issued by a state agency, a city, or a county with its own process, fee, and clock.

The order that saves time

1Find the property's zoning and get a written determination that the use is allowed, or learn that it needs a conditional use permit. Every later record is tied to the address, and the department has said local zoning and permitting must be met before state approval. Zoning
2Form the entity with the Secretary of State and register with Taxation and Revenue; the city and county licenses require both first. Business license
3Get the building permitted, inspected, and occupied: the certificate of occupancy, the fire inspection, and the electrical and HVAC inspection reports come out of the same local process. The facility
4Get the local business license, which in Santa Fe city and county rides on the zoning and fire steps above. Business license
5Draft the attestation and the owner's statement, gather the financial-interest list, and apply. Draft the documents · The application
